How I Work

Engineering Principles & Philosophy

No production system should depend on human memory or manual SSH sessions. When you configure a server by hand, you introduce undocumented drift that inevitably breaks during an outage. I treat infrastructure as software: every VPC, firewall rule, storage mount, and service container is declared in Terraform or SaltStack, tested against staging, and rolled out automatically.

Monitoring is useless if it creates alert fatigue. In my observability setups with Prometheus and Grafana, I focus strictly on actionable signals — saturation thresholds, error rate spikes, disk exhaustion curves, and latency percentiles. Alertmanager routes incidents directly to on-call channels with enough context to diagnose the issue in minutes rather than digging through blind logs.

Bridging DevOps and applied AI requires high engineering rigor. I curate domain datasets from logs and codebase histories to fine-tune open foundation models via LoRA, reducing hallucination. When running autonomous agents on production Linux nodes, they execute under locked-down systemd units and least-privilege service accounts, triaging healthchecks and drafting runbooks safely.

I prioritize lightweight, high-performance tooling across the entire stack. From Alpine-based minimal Docker containers with sub-second startup times to this very site pre-rendered as native static HTML via Rust, I build systems that are fast by default, lean on resource utilization, and trivial to recover when things break.
